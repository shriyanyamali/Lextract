TITLE
Lextract: LLM-Based Extraction of Relevant Market Definitions from European Commission Decisions

ABSTRACT
Relevant market definitions are central to merger and antitrust analysis, but collecting them at scale from European Commission decisions is difficult because the decisions are numerous and heterogeneous in structure and wording. We present Lextract, an open-source pipeline that filters decision PDFs, uses Gemini 2.0 Flash in two calls to locate the market-definition section and to isolate individual definitions, and stores the results as JSON. Applied to 600 cases, it produced 4,105 records that feed JurisMercatus, a semantic-search interface. We document the prompts, settings, exclusion rules and thresholds, give a worked example from source text to record, and combine the model with three symbolic quality checks, namely a structural validity test derived from 50 manually checked definitions, a source-fidelity test that checks whether each record occurs verbatim in its decision, and a manual inspection of 100 records. Of the records, 76.6% pass the structural test. Against all 699 retrievable decisions, 79% of the 4,067 verifiable records are verbatim and 9% near-verbatim. The fidelity test flags the remaining 12% as absent from their source, three quarters of them from short simplified-procedure decisions without market analysis, and removes them in a cleaned release of 3,607 records. The structural test accepts 71% of the flagged records, which shows that checking output against the source text adds information that resemblance-based checks miss. Annotation sheets and baseline scripts for a gold-standard evaluation are provided.
